## Abstract

### English replacement

In multi-story fulfillment warehouses, autonomous mobile robots (AMRs) operate on individual floors and use shared freight elevators for inter-floor transfers. Elevator capacity can become the primary source of delay. Before dispatch begins, wave composition, the choice of which orders to release together, shapes the number, direction, and timing of elevator requests and makespan; to our knowledge, no formulation treats it as a decision variable coupled with shared vertical transport. We formulate wave-release coordination under vertical resource constraints, holding the downstream operational dispatch policy fixed. Each wave is represented by vertical spread, directional imbalance, and temporal clustering. The Bound-and-Gap decomposition partitions composition-related variation in makespan into a structural upper bound that a policy cannot recover at the selected representation resolution and recoverable policy slack; finer wave-profile grouping can only increase the upper bound. Under elevator-model uncertainty, the Model-Dominance Hedge Rule selects the wave-profile class with the smallest makespan under the conservative co-occupancy batching model. Under stated chain-dominance conditions, it minimizes worst-case makespan and coincides with a calibrated distributionally robust solution. In 106,800 pre-registered simulations, dominance holds in 99.2% of matched wave pairs, and the Hedge Rule matches the distributionally robust solution in all six configurations. The decomposition identities hold in all 72 cells, whereas the resolution check is inconclusive only when estimated gaps are small. Across 18 configurations, the capacity-versus-policy conclusion is stable across random seeds and unanimous in 15 of 18 configurations. The tools help operators decide whether to improve wave composition or expand elevator capacity.

## Introduction

### English replacement

Urban land constraints and increasingly short fulfillment promises are encouraging the development of multi-story fulfillment warehouses. In these facilities, autonomous mobile robots (AMRs) move efficiently within each floor, whereas a limited number of shared freight elevators carry all inter-floor traffic. When vertical requests arrive in concentrated bursts, elevator queues delay AMRs and propagate through picking, replenishment, and order completion. Warehouse performance therefore depends not only on how AMRs and elevators are dispatched after work is released, but also on how that work is created before dispatch begins.

A central upstream decision is wave composition: which orders are released together within a wave. A wave determines the floors to be served, the directions and timing of elevator requests, and the degree of concurrent demand imposed on the fleet. It consequently shapes the elevator-trip distribution before any AMR moves. In practice, operators may form waves by arrival order, destination floor, or carrier cutoff. Revising those groupings before release changes neither the warehouse layout nor fleet size, so it is a low-capital way to manage vertical congestion. The operational value of recomposing waves depends on the source of congestion. When elevator capacity is binding, recomposing waves has limited scope to reduce delay. When congestion stems from the pattern of elevator requests, however, a better wave composition can reduce avoidable queueing. Operators therefore need to distinguish these two regimes before choosing between a revised wave-release policy and additional elevator capacity.

Existing studies address important parts of this setting. Order-batching research determines which orders are processed together, multi-story robotic fulfillment studies model vertical transport, and elevator-control studies optimize dispatch under congestion. However, these streams generally fix either the order set, wave content, or transport domain. The unresolved upstream decision is wave composition in a system where a flexible, floor-bound AMR fleet competes for shared freight elevators. Because wave composition shapes the elevator-trip distribution before dispatch begins, it cannot be optimized independently of the vertical bottleneck. Section 2 positions this problem relative to the relevant literature.

We study wave-release coordination as a two-stage decision problem. At the tactical stage, the operator composes a wave; at the operational stage, a fixed operational dispatch policy assigns AMRs and manages elevator trips. We represent each wave by a three-feature wave profile, $\Phi(W)$: vertical spread, directional imbalance, and temporal clustering. This representation supports two tasks: quantifying the variation in makespan that a better wave-release policy can recover and selecting a wave-profile class under elevator-model uncertainty. The Bound-and-Gap decomposition addresses the first task, and the Model-Dominance Hedge Rule addresses the second. Formal definitions, assumptions, and proofs are provided in Sections 3 and 4.

This paper makes three contributions. First, it formalizes the coupled upstream decision involving wave composition, multi-story deployment, a flexible AMR fleet, and shared-elevator capacity. Related studies typically fix wave content or request sets, or confine transport to a single device or tier; our formulation models the coupling explicitly.

Second, we develop two complementary analytical tools for wave-release coordination. The Bound-and-Gap decomposition is diagnostic: it quantifies how much composition-related performance variation is structurally constrained and how much a better wave-release policy can recover. Formally, it separates $GAP$ into a structural upper bound, $H^{\mathrm{up}}$, that a wave-release policy cannot recover at the selected $\Phi$ resolution and recoverable policy slack, $M_{\Phi}$. We show that $M_{\Phi}$ equals the predict-then-optimize decision loss, thereby linking the slack to existing decision-focused methods. A resolution theorem shows that refining the wave-profile grouping can only increase $H^{\mathrm{up}}$. The Model-Dominance Hedge Rule is prescriptive: it selects a robust wave-profile class when the elevator model is uncertain. Under stated dominance conditions, the minimax wave-profile-class selection reduces to the class that is optimal under the conservative co-occupancy batching model and coincides with a calibrated distributionally robust solution. When dominance is only approximate, the analysis provides a computable worst-case-loss bound.

Third, we provide an empirical capacity-versus-policy diagnosis that identifies whether, in a given operating regime, a revised wave-release policy or additional elevator capacity offers the more effective intervention. We evaluate this diagnosis in a pre-registered study of 18 configurations and 106,800 simulations. Under the pre-registered wave grouping and median metric, elevator capacity is the binding constraint in the evaluated settings: approximately 72% of the mean composition-related gap is structural, leaving approximately 28% as recoverable policy slack. The capacity-versus-policy conclusion is stable across random seeds and unanimous in 15 of the 18 configurations. Across the six configurations that directly compare the candidate models, chain dominance holds in 99.2% of matched wave pairs, never falls below 96.0% in an individual cell, and persists as elevator capacity varies from two to five loads per trip. The Hedge Rule selects the same wave-profile class as the calibrated distributionally robust solution in all six configurations. The decomposition identities hold exactly in all 72 experimental cells. Its statistical test detects a nonzero composition-related effect in 57 of the 72 cells; the remaining cells are inconclusive because the estimated effects are small. Together, these results provide a regime-specific basis for selecting between demand shaping through wave composition and capacity expansion.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews related work. Section 3 formulates the problem, Section 4 develops the analytical tools, Section 5 presents the computational study, and Section 6 concludes.

### 中文翻译（仅供理解，不放入英文论文）

城市土地约束以及不断缩短的履约时限，正推动多层履约仓库的发展。在这类设施中，自主移动机器人（AMR）可以在每一楼层内高效移动，而数量有限的共享货运电梯承担全部跨楼层运输。当垂直运输请求集中到达时，电梯排队会延误 AMR，并进一步影响拣选、补货和订单完成。因此，仓库绩效不仅取决于任务释放后如何调度 AMR 和电梯，也取决于在调度开始前如何形成这些任务。

一个核心上游决策是波次组成，即在一个波次中同时释放哪些订单。一个波次决定需要服务的楼层、电梯请求的方向和时序，以及车队面临的并发需求程度。因此，它会在任何 AMR 开始移动之前塑造电梯行程分布。实践中，运营者可能按照到达顺序、目的楼层或承运商截单时间组织波次。在释放前调整这些分组不会改变仓库布局或车队规模，因此是一种管理垂直拥堵的低资本手段。重新组成波次的运行价值取决于拥堵的来源。当电梯容量是约束瓶颈时，重新组成波次对减少延误的作用有限；但当拥堵源于电梯请求的模式时，更优的波次组成能够减少可避免的排队。因此，运营者需要区分这两类运行情形，再决定是修订波次释放策略还是增加电梯容量。

现有研究已经处理了这一场景中的若干重要部分。订单分批研究决定哪些订单被共同处理，多层机器人履约研究刻画垂直运输，电梯控制研究则在拥堵条件下优化调度。然而，这些研究通常将订单集、波次内容或运输范围中的至少一项视为既定。尚未解决的上游决策，是在柔性、受楼层限制的 AMR 车队竞争共享货运电梯的系统中进行波次组成。由于波次组成会在调度开始前塑造电梯行程分布，因此它不能与垂直瓶颈割裂开来单独优化。第 2 节将把这一问题置于相关文献中加以定位。

本文将波次释放协调研究为一个两阶段决策问题。在战术阶段，运营者决定如何组成波次；在运行阶段，固定的运行层调度策略分配 AMR 并管理电梯行程。我们以包含三个特征的波次画像 $\Phi(W)$ 刻画每个波次：垂直跨度、方向失衡和时间聚集度。该表征支持两项任务：量化更优波次释放策略能够恢复的完工期变化，并在电梯模型不确定时选择波次画像类别。Bound-and-Gap 分解处理第一项任务，Model-Dominance Hedge Rule 处理第二项任务。形式化定义、假设和证明将在第 3 节和第 4 节给出。

本文有三项贡献。第一，本文形式化了一个耦合的上游决策，其中包含波次组成、多层部署、柔性 AMR 车队和共享电梯容量。相关研究通常固定波次内容或请求集，或者将运输限制在单一设备或单一层级；本文模型显式刻画这些要素之间的耦合。

第二，本文为波次释放协调提出两项互补的分析工具。Bound-and-Gap 分解是一项诊断性工具：它量化与波次组成相关的绩效变化中有多少受结构性约束，以及有多少能够由更优的波次释放策略恢复。形式上，该分解将 $GAP$ 拆分为结构性上界 $H^{\mathrm{up}}$ 和可恢复的策略余量 $M_{\Phi}$：在所选 $\Phi$ 分辨率下，波次释放策略无法恢复前者。我们证明 $M_{\Phi}$ 等于预测—再优化决策损失，从而将该策略余量与既有的决策导向方法联系起来。一个分辨率定理表明，细化波次画像分组只能提高 $H^{\mathrm{up}}$。Model-Dominance Hedge Rule 是一项决策性工具：当电梯模型不确定时，它选择稳健的波次画像类别。在给定的支配条件下，最小最大波次画像类别选择可化为在保守共乘分批模型下最优的类别，并与经过校准的分布鲁棒解一致。当支配关系仅近似成立时，分析还提供一个可计算的最坏情形损失界。

第三，本文提出一种容量—策略的实证诊断，用于识别在给定运行情形下，修订波次释放策略还是增加电梯容量是更有效的干预措施。我们在一项包含 18 个配置和 106,800 次仿真的预注册研究中评估该诊断。在预注册的波次分组和中位数指标下，电梯容量是所评估情形中的约束瓶颈：平均波次组成相关差距约有 72% 属于结构性部分，约有 28% 为可恢复的策略余量。“容量还是策略更重要”的结论在随机种子之间稳定，并在 18 个配置中的 15 个配置中完全一致。在直接比较候选模型的六个配置中，链式支配关系在 99.2% 的匹配波次对中成立，在任一单元中都不低于 96.0%，并且在每次行程容量从 2 到 5 个载荷变化时仍然成立。Hedge Rule 在全部六个配置中选择的波次画像类别，都与经过校准的分布鲁棒解一致。分解恒等式在全部 72 个实验单元中精确成立。其统计检验在 72 个单元中的 57 个检测到非零的波次组成相关效应；其余单元因估计效应较小而无法得出确定结论。综合来看，这些结果为运营者在“通过波次组成塑造需求”和“扩充容量”之间作出选择提供了按运行情形区分的依据。

## Related Works

### English replacement

Relevant literature for this study comprises three operational streams—wave composition, vertical transport, and downstream dispatch—and two methodological streams—decision loss and information refinement, and model uncertainty. The review first delineates the scope of the three operational streams and then discusses the methodological foundations for the two analytical tools used in this study. Across these streams, the upstream coupling between wave composition and shared, multi-story elevator capacity appears to receive limited direct attention. Recent surveys described the broader warehouse landscape, including the evolution of warehousing research, e-commerce fulfillment, robotized warehouse systems, and order-batching problems (Boysen and de Koster, 2025; Boysen, de Koster, and Weidinger, 2019; Azadeh, De Koster, and Roy, 2019; Pardo et al., 2024).

First, warehouse-operations research examined wave composition as a decision problem, but did not model its consequences for shared-elevator demand in a flexible AMR fleet. Representative studies addressed wave picking and walk-and-pick systems (Gademann et al., 2001; Bozer and Kile, 2008; Bartholdi and Hackman, 2019), multiple-picker makespan minimization (Ardjmand et al., 2018), and e-commerce order-line batching and scheduling (Rasmi et al., 2022; Schiffer et al., 2022; Haouassi et al., 2022). AMR-assisted studies jointly considered order batching, assignment, and routing (Scholz, Schubert, and Wäscher, 2017; Žulj et al., 2022), while Qin, Kang, and Yang (2024) examined wave cardinality in a multi-tote AMR system. Device-level work considered batching across vertical lift modules and order-spread sequencing in automated sorting (Lenoble, Frein, and Hammami, 2018; Boysen, Fedtke, and Weidinger, 2018), and recent robotic mobile fulfillment system (RMFS) batching work focused primarily on single-tier throughput (e.g., Liu et al., 2025). These studies provided important foundations for wave composition, but they did not address how it changes demand for elevators shared by a flexible AMR fleet across multiple floors. In contrast, this study represents wave composition with a wave profile, $\Phi(W)$, whose features describe the resulting vertical-trip demand.

Second, research on multi-story robotic mobile fulfillment systems (RMFSs) and multi-tier shuttle systems modeled vertical transport, but typically treated the task set as given before operational decisions were optimized. Examples included inbound-job scheduling in four-way shuttle systems (Z. Wu et al., 2024), order and pod assignment in multi-level RMFSs (Tadumadze et al., 2023), queueing models for RMFSs and compact storage systems with lifts (Lamballais, Roy, and De Koster, 2017; Tappia et al., 2017), and retrieval scheduling under two lifts (Chen et al., 2023). J. Wu et al. (2025) jointly optimized picking and replenishment within a tier. Wang et al. (2025) provided a closely related exception by jointly scheduling retrieval requests, shuttles, and two shared lift types in a multi-tier shuttle system. Nevertheless, their retrieval-request set was specified in advance. The present study instead treats wave composition as the upstream tactical decision that shapes vertical congestion before a fixed operational dispatch policy is applied.

Third, research closest to the present physical setting optimized elevator or robot operations under exogenous demand rather than altering that demand through wave composition. Chakravarty et al. (2025) formulated multi-robot lift scheduling with Boolean satisfiability; Tsai et al. (2025) optimized elevator standby strategies; and Richer, Bierlaire, and Torres (2024) studied static elevator dispatching with destination control. A parallel literature used reinforcement learning for downstream scheduling and control, from elevator group control (Crites and Barto, 1998) to traffic-aware elevator dispatching (Wan, Lee, and Shin, 2024), batch-order scheduling in RMFSs (Cheng et al., 2024), multi-robot task allocation (Ma et al., 2025; Dhanaraj et al., 2025; Wen and Ma, 2024), and AMR-fleet control (Wesselhöft et al., 2022). These approaches learned or optimized an operational dispatch policy after demand was given, whereas the present framework changes the demand pattern through wave composition. The approaches are therefore complementary.

The Bound-and-Gap decomposition draws on a methodological stream that examined decision loss and information refinement. Predict-then-optimize research examined the loss incurred when a learned prediction drove a downstream optimization decision (Elmachtoub and Grigas, 2022; Vera et al., 2021; Chenreddy et al., 2022), while decision-focused learning and generalization studies characterized how such loss could be reduced (Mandi et al., 2024; El Balghiti et al., 2023). The Bound-and-Gap decomposition connects this decision-loss perspective to recoverable policy slack in wave composition. Its representation-resolution component is also related to classical work on information refinement and partition-based stochastic optimization (Blackwell, 1953; Song and Luedtke, 2015): a more refined wave-profile class can reveal additional decision-relevant variation.

The Model-Dominance Hedge Rule draws on a complementary methodological stream that addressed decisions under model uncertainty. Wasserstein distributionally robust optimization addresses uncertainty by optimizing against adverse probability distributions within a specified ambiguity set (Mohajerin Esfahani and Kuhn, 2018; Blanchet and Murthy, 2019; Gao and Kleywegt, 2023), building on broader robust-optimization foundations (Delage and Ye, 2010; Bertsimas, Brown, and Caramanis, 2011). Sadana et al. (2025) placed these approaches within the broader contextual-optimization literature. The Model-Dominance Hedge Rule draws on this perspective to select a wave-profile class when the elevator model is uncertain. Finally, robust scheduling under model uncertainty usually addresses parametric variation within an agreed model family (Lu and Shen, 2021; Wiesemann et al., 2013). Here, the uncertainty is structural: the throughput abstraction and the conservative co-occupancy batching model represent different ways of modelling elevator trips. This distinction motivates a rule based on model dominance rather than parameter estimation.

Taken together, these streams leave unresolved the upstream coupling between wave composition and shared vertical capacity. This study integrates these components by treating wave composition, multi-story deployment, flexible AMR fleets, and shared elevator capacity as one coupled decision setting. It thereby complements downstream scheduling methods with an upstream, interpretable basis for deciding when and how to modify the demand entering the vertical transport system.

### 中文翻译（仅供理解，不放入英文论文）

与本研究相关的文献包括三条运行决策文献线——波次组成、垂直运输和下游调度——以及两条方法学文献线——决策损失与信息细化、模型不确定性。下文先梳理三条运行决策文献线的研究范围，随后讨论支撑本文两个分析工具的两条方法学文献线。在这些文献线中，波次组成与多层仓库中共享电梯容量之间的上游耦合似乎只得到有限的直接关注。近期综述分别刻画了仓储研究的发展、电子商务履约、机器人化仓储系统以及订单分批问题的整体图景（Boysen and de Koster, 2025; Boysen, de Koster, and Weidinger, 2019; Azadeh, De Koster, and Roy, 2019; Pardo et al., 2024）。

第二，多层机器人移动履约系统（RMFS）和多层穿梭车系统研究刻画了垂直运输，但通常在优化运行层决策之前将任务集视为既定。例如，相关研究包括四向穿梭车系统中的入库任务调度（Z. Wu et al., 2024）、多层 RMFS 中的订单与货架分配（Tadumadze et al., 2023）、RMFS 与配备升降机的紧凑型仓储系统的排队模型（Lamballais, Roy, and De Koster, 2017; Tappia et al., 2017），以及双升降机条件下的取货请求调度（Chen et al., 2023）。J. Wu et al. (2025) 在单层内联合优化拣选和补货。Wang et al. (2025) 是一个与本文较接近的例外：其在多层穿梭车系统中联合调度取货请求、穿梭车和两类共享升降机。然而，该研究的取货请求集合仍是预先给定的。本文则将波次组成视为上游战术决策，使其在固定的运行层调度策略执行之前就塑造垂直拥堵。

第三，与本文物理场景最接近的研究在外生需求条件下优化电梯或机器人运行，而不是通过波次组成改变该需求。Chakravarty et al. (2025) 使用布尔可满足性方法建立多机器人升降机调度模型；Tsai et al. (2025) 优化电梯待命策略；Richer, Bierlaire, and Torres (2024) 研究目的地控制下的静态电梯调度。另一条平行研究路线使用强化学习处理下游调度和控制问题，涵盖电梯群控（Crites and Barto, 1998）、感知交通模式的电梯调度（Wan, Lee, and Shin, 2024）、RMFS 中的批量订单调度（Cheng et al., 2024）、多机器人任务分配（Ma et al., 2025; Dhanaraj et al., 2025; Wen and Ma, 2024）以及 AMR 车队控制（Wesselhöft et al., 2022）。这些方法在需求既定后学习或优化运行调度策略，而本文通过波次组成改变该策略所面对的需求模式，因此两类方法是互补的。

Bound-and-Gap 分解借鉴了一条研究决策损失和信息细化的方法学文献线。预测后优化研究关注学习得到的预测被送入下游优化决策时产生的损失（Elmachtoub and Grigas, 2022; Vera et al., 2021; Chenreddy et al., 2022）；决策导向学习和泛化研究则讨论如何降低此类损失（Mandi e atl., 2024; El Balghiti et al., 2023）。本文的 Bound-and-Gap 分解将这一决策损失视角与波次组成中的可恢复策略余量联系起来。其关于表征分辨率的部分也与信息细化和基于划分的随机优化的经典研究相关（Blackwell, 1953; Song and Luedtke, 2015）：更精细的波次画像类别可以揭示更多与决策相关的变化。

Model-Dominance Hedge Rule 借鉴了一条处理模型不确定性下决策问题的互补方法学文献线。Wasserstein 分布鲁棒优化通过在给定模糊集合内针对不利的概率分布进行优化来应对不确定性（Mohajerin Esfahani and Kuhn, 2018; Blanchet and Murthy, 2019; Gao and Kleywegt, 2023），其基础来自更广泛的鲁棒优化理论（Delage and Ye, 2010; Bertsimas, Brown, and Caramanis, 2011）。Sadana et al. (2025) 将这些方法置于更广义的情境优化文献中。本文的 Model-Dominance Hedge Rule 借鉴这一视角，在电梯模型不确定时选择波次画像类别。最后，模型不确定性下的鲁棒调度通常处理的是同一既定模型族内部的参数变化（Lu and Shen, 2021; Wiesemann et al., 2013）。本文面对的则是结构性不确定性：吞吐量抽象模型与保守的共乘分批模型以不同方式描述电梯行程。因此，本文采用基于模型支配关系的规则，而不是依赖参数估计。

综合来看，这些文献线仍未解决波次组成与共享垂直容量之间的上游耦合。本文将波次组成、多层部署、柔性 AMR 车队和共享电梯容量视为同一耦合决策场景，从而以一种上游且可解释的方式补充下游调度方法，用于判断何时以及如何改变进入垂直运输系统的需求。
