## One change from the judge table

The judge read the 11 s "Elevator opening/closing delays" of Bhosekar et al. (2020, arXiv:2006.10031, Table 3) as the whole door block (opening, closing delay and closing) and asked the author to check the source. The assistant opened it on 2026-09-27: page 10 describes the value as "the time it takes to open and close the door". Read literally, it is opening plus closing, so the documented closing delay (2.0 s, Sorsa 2019) is added: the door block's upper bound becomes 13.0 s, the loading range 8.3 to 22.0 s (midpoint 15.15 s) and the unloading range 7.8 to 21.61 s (midpoint 14.705 s). This follows the judge's own rule of never narrowing a range below the evidence. The judge's alternative figures (22.15 and 21.76 s) imply 2.15 s for the added component; the largest closing delay in the verified evidence is 2.0 s (Sorsa 2019, item kone-17), which is used here.

- No measured AMR drive-in or drive-out time exists for a freight lift. The 2.4 s value is an assumed figure for a light robot, and 8 s is an ideal design value for a passenger-lift robot. - Floor height is the weakest gamma input. No Asian floor-to-floor height for a warehouse with freight elevators is unambiguous (Goodman's label '樓高' is translated as ceiling height), and there are no data for the Quicktron Nanchang case. The 9.75 m high end is one building's Level 1, applied to both gaps.
- Freight-lift acceleration, jerk and levelling are undocumented; passenger values are used. The low jerk of 0.51 m/s3 comes from a single measured 3.97 m/s passenger lift.
- Model interactions to disclose in the case description. In M2 the co-boarding window equals tau^L, so raising load_time from 2 to about 14 s widens batching opportunities about sevenfold. The second co-rider's transfer is not charged. Repositioning flights carry no start or levelling delay. The linear gamma leaves about ±0.65 s error per trip.

## Judge's cross-checks (verbatim list)

- Arithmetic re-computed in Python for this ruling (no files written). gamma corners: 6.758 s (h 5.48, v 1.0, a 0.8, j 1.2) and 20.856 s (h 9.75, v 0.5, a 0.4, j 0.51). Synthesis A's corners reproduce at 6.841 and 20.600 s, so A's arithmetic is correct and only its inputs differ.
- Regime check for the Peters formula. The largest threshold (a^2 v + v^2 j)/(j a) over the corners is about 2.8 m, below the smallest floor height of 5.48 m. So the rated-speed branch t_f = d/v + a/j + v/a is valid for every 1-floor and 2-floor flight.
- Formula versus tabulated values. At 3.3 m, the ideal formula gives flight times 0.4 to 2.9 s below the Barney/Peters table values (7.0 s at 1.0 m/s; 10.0 s below 1.0 m/s). The tables include start and levelling delays, which this mapping charges in tau^L (0.5 to 1.0 s) and tau^U (0 to 0.61 s). gamma is therefore not inflated; if anything it is slightly optimistic.
- Linearisation error. With gamma = [t_f(h) + t_f(2h)]/3, a 1-floor flight is under-charged by 0.64 s at the fast corner (8.6% of 7.40 s) and 0.68 s at the slow corner (3.1% of 21.53 s); a 2-floor flight is over-charged by the same amount. The total over the 1:1 loaded mix is exact (peters_rtt-4 non-linearity).
- Convexity. gamma at the central inputs (h 7.615 m, v 0.75 m/s, a 0.55 m/s2, j 0.855 m/s3) is 11.49 s, below the range midpoint of 13.81 s, because gamma is convex in 1/v. The protocol rule (midpoint of the documented range) is applied as written and gives a somewhat slower elevator.
- Speed-band sensitivity, not adopted. Admitting 1.6 m/s (Schindler 2600 rope up to 2000 kg; KONE TranSys EU) lowers gamma_low to 5.20 s. Admitting TKE EXPANSION traction at 0.3 m/s raises gamma_high to 33.5 s. The 0.5 to 1.0 m/s band is a case specification backed by Barney Table 5.3 (travel under 20 m -> under 1.00 m/s) and KONE TranSys DX standard speeds.
- Double counting check. Door opening and closing appear once per stop, at the origin in tau^L and at the destination in tau^U. The photocell or closing delay appears once per stop, inside D; under the lumped 11 s reading it is not added again. Start delay appears only in tau^L, levelling delay only in tau^U, and neither is in gamma. Repositioning flights carry neither, which understates them by 0.5 to 1.6 s (a model limit).
- Implied loaded trip, tau^L + n*gamma + tau^U (low / mid / high). One floor: 22.9 / 41.7 / 60.5 s. Two floors: 29.6 / 55.5 / 81.3 s. The mid two-floor trip (55.5 s) is consistent with the BCO goods-lift guidance of 60 s from loading bay to top floor (peters_rtt-17). The case lies between lumped simulation values (RAWSim-O 10 s per floor; hospital model 2.7 to 3.2 s per level) and measured robot rides in shared passenger lifts (86 to 374 s including waiting, warehouse_floors-17).
- Performance-time plausibility, passenger reference at 3.3 m and 1.0 m/s. t_f(1) + t_sd + t_o + t_c = (5.1 to 6.6) + (0.5 to 1.0) + (4.5 to 8.15) = about 10 to 16 s. BS 5655-6 gives 8/10/12 s for excellent/average/poor, and CIBSE Table 3.8 says to consider replacement above 12 s. The low end sits at 'average' passenger practice and the high end is slower, as expected for wider freight doors.
- Door-block plausibility. The documented component sum (t_o + t_c + t_cd) is 5.4 to 10.15 s. Author-derived freight-door strokes at the VA-required 0.3 m/s (schindler_other-17) give roughly 6 to 10 s per open-close pair. The AGV handbook gives 11 s (warehouse_floors-13). Using 11 s as the upper bound of D is consistent with both.
- Drive-in plausibility. Author arithmetic: car depth 2.4 to 4.0 m (kone-8; Meitetsu 4.0 m) at the ASME-derived 0.365 to 0.5 m/s transfer limit (peters_rtt-16) gives 4.8 to 11 s. This overlaps the documented 2.4 to 8.0 s. The documented high of 8 s may be low for heavy AMRs in deep cars, and the 2.4 s low implies about 1 m/s entry speed.
- Duty ratings, order of magnitude only (ratings are not cycle times). At midpoints a continuously busy car completes at most about 86 one-floor loaded trips per hour, within the Schindler rope rating of 180 rides/h but above the hydraulic 30/60 rides/h. This supports the traction assumption. At the low end (about 157 trips/h) the Otis Robusta rating of 90 starts/h would be exceeded.
- Against the synthetic defaults (5, 2, 2, 5 s). gamma rises from 5 to 13.81 s (the default lies below the documented low of 6.76 s). load_time rises from 2 to 14.15 s and unload_time from 2 to 13.705 s; 2 s is roughly one door stroke, far below the documented lows of 8.3 and 7.8 s. service_time rises from 5 to 10.5 s, and the default of 5 s lies inside 1 to 20 s. The ratio (tau^L + tau^U)/gamma goes from 0.8 to about 2.0.

Midpoint gamma: A 13.72, B 13.85, judge 13.81. load_time: A and B 13.725, judge 14.15. unload_time: A 13.28, B 12.975, judge 13.705. service_time: A 10.5, B 4.5, judge 10.5.
